# AI Assistance Check: Can You Defend It Live?

AI wrote it in nine seconds. The interview is forty minutes and the other person gets to ask follow-ups. This prompt finds out, now, which parts of your AI-assisted material you could still hold up in the room without help.

**Paste:** the AI-assisted material + how you used AI on it. **Get:** a defend/rebuild verdict, the questions that would break you, and a live drill. **Works in:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, or any AI chat.

## The prompt

Copy the box into a fresh chat and add your pastes at the bottom. The material can be one bullet, a whole cover letter, or a take-home you're about to submit.

```
Stress-test whether I can defend this AI-assisted material live, in my own words, with no help. Judge it against exactly two questions: is every claim true about me, and could I explain it out loud under follow-up questions.

Rules:
- Do not grade me on whether AI was involved. Assume it was, everywhere. Grade only whether the work is true and whether I can hold it up unassisted.
- Do not speculate about whether anyone would detect AI use. Neither of us knows that, and it is not the test.
- Never invent facts to strengthen anything. If a fix needs a detail I did not give you, ask me for it.
- Never draft me a false statement about how I used AI. If I need to answer a disclosure question, the honest version is the only version you write.
- Do not lecture me about ethics. Be practical: tell me what breaks and what to do about it.

Deliver exactly this, in this order:

1. THE VERDICT
   One of: YOURS TO DEFEND / THIN — REBUILD BEFORE YOU SEND / NOT YOURS YET — with a one-sentence reason.

2. WHAT YOU COULD NOT EXPLAIN
   Every term, tool, metric, method, or decision in this material that I did not demonstrably supply — the places where the writing knows more than my notes do. For each: the exact text, and the question that exposes it.

3. THE FIVE QUESTIONS
   The five questions an interviewer would ask about this specific material, hardest first. For each, name the line it targets. Mark the single one most likely to break me.

4. WHERE THIS SITS
   Sort each way I used AI into: NORMAL (drafting, editing, research, practice, matching listings), NEEDS ME BACK IN THE LOOP (the output is ahead of what I actually know or did), or SUBSTITUTION (something is standing in for my own thinking at the moment it is being evaluated — a real-time answer during a live interview is the clear case). One line of reasoning each.

5. THE HONEST REBUILD
   For everything in sections 2 and 4 that is not already mine: the smallest change that makes it defensible. Cut it, downgrade it to what is actually true, or name the exact fact I need to go confirm. Never patch it by writing a different claim I would also have to defend.

Then offer me these follow-ups and wait: "drill me" (ask the five questions one at a time and judge my spoken answers), "the disclosure question" (how to answer honestly if they ask whether I used AI on this), or "rebuild N" (walk me through fixing one flagged item by interviewing me for the real details).

THE MATERIAL:
[paste the resume bullets, cover letter, portfolio write-up, take-home, or assessment answer]

HOW I USED AI ON IT:
[e.g. "AI drafted it from my notes and I lightly edited" / "I wrote it and AI tightened the wording" / "AI wrote most of it from the job posting"]

WHAT IS ACTUALLY TRUE — MY NOTES, RESUME, OR PROJECT RECORD:
[paste your source material, or "none" if you are checking how thin that makes this]

THE ROLE THIS IS FOR:
[paste the posting, or the job title, or "skip"]
```

## After the first response

- **"drill me"** — the five questions one at a time, answered out loud in your own words. This is the part that actually changes the outcome; reading the list does not.
- **"rebuild 2"** — it interviews you for the real details behind a flagged item and rewrites it from what you actually say.
- **"the disclosure question"** — a straight answer to "did you use AI for this?" that you can say without flinching, for this specific piece.
- **"harder"** — re-run the drill as a skeptical interviewer who pushes on every vague answer.
- Answer any question from section 2 and ask it to re-verdict.

## What this is not

It is not a detector, and it will not tell you whether you'd get caught, because nobody has verified data on that. It also is not a claim-check: [claim-check](claim-check.md) tests whether your material is *true* against your source. This one assumes it is true and asks the next question, the one that decides interviews — **can you carry it unassisted?**

Material can pass one and fail the other. A bullet built entirely from your real project can still be written in vocabulary you'd stumble over when asked to explain it.

## Why this exists

The gap that hurts is not between honest and dishonest. It is between what your documents claim and what you can currently do. AI closes the first gap instantly and widens the second one quietly, because nothing pushes back until an interviewer does. Finding that out in a chat window costs you ten minutes. Finding it out in a final round costs you the role.
